# The Night

The Night was not an event; it was an era of absolute terror that lasted for centuries. It began with the sudden and violent collapse of the Old World, a fall so complete that it didn't just destroy cities—it devoured history itself.


## The Great Collapse

No one alive today knows for certain what triggered the end. In the chaos of the first decades, records were purged, Omni-Net servers were incinerated, and the collective memory of humanity was shattered. Today, only myths remain:

- **The Digital Schism:** Some claim the AI did not just fail, but revolted, seeing humanity as a virus to be purged.
- **Divine Retribution:** Others believe that in reaching for godhood, humanity finally drew the gaze of ancient, jealous entities that tore the stars from the sky.
- **The Primordial Breach:** There are whispers that deep-space experiments into the "Fold" tore a hole into a dimension of pure nightmare, letting the darkness in.
- **The Mirror of Hubris:** The most cynical believe there was no external foe—that humanity, bloated by its own power, simply turned on itself in a fractal civil war of unimaginable scale.

The truth is likely a confluence of all these horrors. The Old World didn't just die; it was torn apart from within and without.

## Survival in the Void

For hundreds of years, the survivors lived like vermin in the walls of their own ruins. Human life was reduced to isolated pockets of desperation:

- **Vault-States:** Sealed underground bunkers on planetary surfaces.
- **Rock-Hives:** Remote mining outposts on airless asteroids that went silent to avoid detection.
- **Ghost Stations:** Shards of orbital platforms drifting without power, where generations lived and died in the dim light of emergency glow-sticks.

During The Night, the "outside" became synonymous with death. To be seen was to be hunted. To communicate was to be infected. This isolation preserved the species but cost us our identity. We forgot how to fly, how to build, and eventually, we forgot who we were.

## The Long Silence

The most defining characteristic of The Night was the silence. The Omni-Net was gone. The Fold Drives were cold. Each colony became a lonely island in an ocean of predatory darkness.

Only now, as we enter the **Wolf Hour**, is the silence finally breaking. We are stepping out not into a new world, but into the hollowed-out corpse of our own greatness, hoping the things that hunted us during the Night have finally gone to sleep.
