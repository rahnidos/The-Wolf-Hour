# The Galaxy Today

The Milky Way is a barred spiral galaxy. This vast region of the universe can accommodate any possible campaign. In reality, however, the game takes place among the ruins of the [Old World](Old%20World.md). Before the catastrophe, the colonized bubble consisted of approximately fifteen thousand star systems. Nevertheless, nothing prevents you from moving the game beyond the old bubble if you feel the need.

Before the fall, humanity inhabited a massive expanse. Technology allowed for the colonization of not only Earth-like planets but essentially any celestial body capable of supporting a residential habitat. Space was also filled with orbital stations and massive residential ships. The remains of all this are still accessible today.

The Solar System, the cradle of humanity, was the central hub of the bubble. The bubble itself was approximately five hundred light-years in diameter. Of course, population density was not uniform. The center of the bubble was dense with civilization, while the periphery was filled with small mining settlements or fully automated factories.

## The Shattered Map

Today, there is no such thing as a "galaxy-wide map." The Omni-Net is dead, and with it, the real-time positioning of stars and stations vanished. For a modern traveler, the galaxy consists only of their home system and perhaps one or two "Clean Routes" leading to neighbors. Everything else is the **Great Unknown**.

The old bubble has dissolved into thousands of isolated "Islands of Light" separated by vast, dark reaches of space. Most systems that were once colonized are now **Dead Systems**—silent graveyards of billion-ton structures where the air ran out centuries ago.

## The Silence of the Core

While the center of the bubble was once the heart of humanity, it is now the most avoided region in existence. The catastrophe began there, and the "echoes" of the Night are still strongest in the Core. Those few who have ventured toward the inner systems speak of "Ghost Signals" on their sensors and massive, dark shapes that do not belong to any known ship. It is a region of high-tech hauntings where the laws of physics and logic seem most frayed.

## A Lawless Frontier

In the Wolf Hour, you are truly on your own. There is no central authority to call for help, no galactic police, and no unified currency. Trust is the rarest resource. When two ships meet in the void, it is a moment of extreme tension; they might exchange vital air filters for fuel, or they might open fire to steal each other’s scrap. Every encounter is a gamble between survival and extinction.
