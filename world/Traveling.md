# Traveling through the galaxy

The only means of travel in the universe are spaceships. Currently, no new ships are produced; there are no functioning shipyards, nor is there the knowledge or technology to build them. The only flying ships are those that have been found, and they are not always fully functional.

Based on size and function, ships are divided into the following types:

- [Scout](app://obsidian.md/Scout.md)
- Medium
- Heavy
- Transport / Cruise Liner
- Carrier
- Mothership

Ships larger than the Heavy type are unable to land on planets or moons; they are equipped with landers. Even Heavy ships do not always handle atmospheric flight well.

## Three Flight Modes

There are three primary models of travel: short-range flight, intra-system movement, and long-distance travel beyond the star system.

**Short-range flight** is powered by traditional jet engines. These are low-speed trips, mostly within the vicinity of a planet and its satellites. All ships are capable of this, though not all can land. This category includes landing maneuvers and docking. The required skill is [Piloting](app://obsidian.md/Piloting.md).

For further travel, a ship must be equipped with a **Jump Drive**. This drive operates in two modes: Superluminal and Hyperspace.

**Superluminal Mode** is used for traveling within a star system. It allows acceleration to approximately fifty times the speed of light. The ship requires time for this, as it must begin braking maneuvers roughly halfway to the destination. To enter superluminal mode, a ship must be at least 5 km away from any large mass. Preparation takes about 5 minutes. This requires the [Piloting](app://obsidian.md/Piloting.md) skill.

**Hyperspace Mode** allows jumps between systems. In the Old World, AI calculated these parameters instantly, restricted only by fuel and range. Today, the reality is much more dangerous:

- **The Fall of AI:** During the Night, a wave of madness and viral corruption decimated AI systems. Most ship-bound intelligences turned "feral" or became hosts for devastating digital plagues. 
- **Jump Data:** Most modern ships rely on "Clean Routes"—pre-calculated coordinates recovered from ancient systems or laboriously verified by Astrogators. This data is one of the most valuable commodities in the Wolf Hour.
- **Isolated Systems:** To prevent infection, ship computers are now completely air-gapped and isolated from one another. All navigational data must be entered manually.
- **Communication Hazards:** Onboard communication modules are frequently disabled or physically removed, as they are the primary vectors for feral AI and viral intrusion. Long-range digital communication is considered a high-risk gamble.
- **Astrogation:** Calculating a new jump from scratch without a Clean Route is a grueling and high-stakes task. It requires the [Astrogation](app://obsidian.md/Astrogation.md) skill and carries a significant risk of ending up in deep space or worse.
- **Fuel:** Jumps consume large amounts of fuel depending on ship size. A ship's range is usually measured in light-years per full tank.
## Maintenance and Repairs

Since no new ships are being produced, every flying vessel is a relic.
- **Cannibalization:** Repairs often require "cannibalizing" other wrecks. It is common to see a Scout ship with a patchwork of plating or jury-rigged components from entirely different models.
- **The "Feel" of the Ship:** Without AI to monitor micro-fractures or system stresses, pilots must rely on manual gauges, vibrations, and the literal sound of the engines. A ship is a mechanical beast that needs constant attention.

## Life Support

In the Old World, life support was a perfect, invisible background process. Today, it is a constant source of anxiety.
- **Air and Pressure:** Air recyclers and thermal regulators are prone to failure. Many crews wear light flight suits at all times, distrusting the ship's ability to maintain pressure.
- **Manual Overrides:** Most modern crews have installed physical levers and analog valves to bypass the automated systems that the AI once controlled, ensuring that a software glitch won't accidentally vent the atmosphere.
## Fuel and Refueling

Ships may be equipped with small refineries that produce fuel from hydrogen (collected near gas giants) or water. If a refinery works perfectly—which is rare—filling the tanks takes about five hours.

Ships without refineries must use planetary facilities or scavenge fuel from abandoned orbital stations and service outposts on asteroids and moons.
